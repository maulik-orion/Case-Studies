Real Constraints Behind This Result
Scenario 6's EBITDA gain is not free. Production and cold-storage capacity remain binding: even after the price-driven demand pullback, 450.7K liters still go unmet (down from 1,601.7K in Base Case) — the plant physically cannot serve everyone. Hedging coverage is at its practical ceiling (100% of dairy fat); no further cost protection is available without a new instrument. Contractual pricing with large accounts may cap how much of the modeled increase is achievable — this scenario assumes full pass-through. The trade-off: Scenario 6 gives up 862.6K liters of volume and $2.2M of revenue versus Base Case and target, respectively, to capture materially higher margin.

Key Actions
What management controls now, what it doesn't, and what the board should weigh for beyond FY2026.

Short Term — Under Management Control

→Raise price 5%/month, Apr–DecCompounds to +55% by December. The single largest lever, worth $5.3M of the $6.25M FY EBITDA gain, subject to contract and competitive limits.
→Reallocate marketing spendDown ~68% Apr–Jul, redeployed above-plan into Aug–Dec (net −16% FY, $3.16M vs. $3.77M planned); worth $651K. The Aug increase lands in a still-constrained month, worth re-checking.
→Cut trade promotion to the floor2% off list, 1 promoted week, all of Apr–Dec. Biggest cuts are Oct–Nov (was 4.5%, 2 weeks) despite no capacity constraint there; worth $233.5K.
→Extend dairy fat hedging to 100%Up from 40%, locked at the same index (108.5) already secured on the first tranche; worth $65.8K, the smallest but safest lever.

Short Term — Not Under Management Control

→Summer temperatureWarmer weather lifts demand further, but the plant is already capacity-constrained in peak months — hotter summers mostly convert into more unmet demand, not more revenue.

Long Term — For the Board to Consider

→Capex for a 4th lineAdds 300K–500K liters of monthly capacity to capture demand the plant currently turns away.
→Shift mix toward Zero & premiumAnalyze whether these lines carry better margin per unit of scarce capacity.
→Pre-build ahead of peak seasonUse Jan–Mar capacity headroom to build inventory before the summer shortfall hits.
→Consider third-party cold storageIf owned freezer capacity (620K liters) can't support a larger pre-build program.

CFO Simulator: The Business
Explore the FY2026 model hands-on. This primer sets up the drivers before you move any levers.

Polar Peak is a mid-market ice cream manufacturer selling through Retail, Foodservice, and Export channels. Q1 2026 came in 10% ahead of budget on an unusually mild spring, and the board needs to know how much of that is durable versus weather. The FY2026 forecast models nine remaining months (Apr–Dec) using a driver-based approach, not a black box, built on five factors: temperature (volume rises with summer heat), pricing (elasticity by channel), marketing spend (diminishing returns), trade promotion (discount depth and duration), and production capacity (a hard ceiling the plant cannot exceed).

What Drives the Forecast

  • TemperatureWarmer summers (Jun–Aug) lift volume most; shoulder months (Apr, May, Sep, Oct) react less; winter is roughly flat.
  • PricingPrice increases raise revenue per unit but reduce volume, and the trade-off differs by channel (Retail customers are more price-sensitive than Foodservice).
  • MarketingSpend lifts demand, but with diminishing returns; doubling the budget doesn't double the effect.
  • Trade SpendRetailer discounts move volume, but every point of discount also cuts the price on units that would have sold anyway.
  • CapacityThe plant has a hard monthly production ceiling. In peak summer months, demand already exceeds what can be made, a real constraint, not a modeling choice.
  • HedgingA share of dairy fat costs is locked at a fixed price through December. Spot dairy costs are expected to keep rising, so more coverage protects margin.

Five FY2026 Scenarios

Base Case$5.3M EBITDA
Upside, Heatwave$4.4M EBITDA
Downside, Cool & Wet$6.0M EBITDA
Input Cost Shock$3.9M EBITDA
Macro Recession$5.5M EBITDA

Key Risks

  • Production capacity is the binding constraint most months; price and volume plans can't outrun what the plant can physically make.
  • The Heatwave scenario has the lowest EBITDA of the upside cases, since hot weather also raises dairy input costs.
  • Several elasticity assumptions (channel-level pricing, launch cannibalization) could not be independently validated against three years of history.
